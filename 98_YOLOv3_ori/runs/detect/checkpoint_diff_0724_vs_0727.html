What this method cannot do

This compares rendered JPEGs, not detections. Neither output folder was written with save_txt, so no box coordinates or confidence values exist on disk — the numbers below are measured from pixels, and the per-frame readings are transcribed by eye off the images.

It can therefore tell you where and how much the two checkpoints disagree. It cannot tell you which one is more accurate. No precision, no recall, no mAP. Every judgement in the evidence section below is a visual impression from a single frame, not a measurement.

Finding

0727 is not simply "better" or "more conservative" than 0724. Across the ten most-changed frames it wins four, regresses on three, and does nothing at all on three.

The single most reliable pattern is confidence drift upward on detections both checkpoints already agree on — 0.71→0.78, 0.83→0.86, 0.88→0.89. That trend is mild and near-universal, and it is also the least interesting kind of change, because it alters no decision.

Where behaviour actually changes, it changes inconsistently. 0727 cleanly suppresses duplicate boxes on frame 01 and adds a genuinely missed bird on frame 09 — then produces extra duplicates on frames 03 and 05, and drops most of a distant flock on frame 02. An earlier read of mine that 0727 trades recall for precision does not survive contact with all ten frames: it goes both ways.

Distribution

Change is heavily concentrated. The curve falls off fast after frame 10, and the bottom half of the set is confidence digits repainting by a hundredth. Ranking by this number is how the ten frames below were chosen — it identifies where to look, not how big the behavioural difference is.

How the diff is computed

Four mechanics in 03_Compare_Runs.py do the real work. Everything else is file matching, sorting and layout.

CAST

Promote to int16 before subtracting

The arrays are uint8. 10 - 200 wraps to 66 instead of -190, so without the cast every large decrease silently becomes a small positive number and the whole diff is garbage. This one line is load-bearing.

MAX

Collapse RGB by maximum, not mean

Per pixel, take the largest of the three channel deltas. A blue box appearing on grey sky is a near-pure single-channel change — averaging would dilute it to a third of its true magnitude.

Δ>30

Threshold at 30 to absorb JPEG noise

Both folders hold re-encoded JPEGs, so visually identical pixels still differ by ±2–5. Below about 10 that compression noise reads as signal; above about 60 the anti-aliased box edges stop registering.

MAX3

Dilate the mask before downscaling

A 3×3 maximum filter grows each changed region by one pixel. Box outlines are 1–3px wide and vanish when the sheet is viewed zoomed out. Dilating at full resolution and shrinking afterwards is the order that preserves them.

Known defects in the script

The "identical" column is mislabelled

It is set from not mask.any() — meaning no pixel crossed the threshold, not that the files match. The terminal prints it as "byte-visually identical", a contradictory phrase that is neither byte-level nor an identity check. It read 0/40 here so nothing downstream was affected, but it will mislead on a future pair.

Memory scales with folder size, not with --top

Both decoded images and the mask are retained for every frame, not just the ones rendered. Fine at 40 frames; it will exhaust RAM at a few thousand. The fix is to keep only metrics on the first pass and re-open the top N when rendering.

Stale --out help text

The help string advertises <run_b>_vs_<run_a>_compare; the actual default is compare_<a>_vs_<b>. Cosmetic, no functional effect.
